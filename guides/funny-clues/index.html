THE ALIBI PLAYBOOK

Suspicious is a delivery style.

Published by Imposter Play · How this guide is made

A good clue makes the people who know the word nod, and leaves the imposter with just enough room to panic. Funny clues work best when they are unexpected rather than impossible.

Choose an association, not a definition

For “popcorn,” “premiere” points to the cinema without naming the snack. For “umbrella,” “forecast” works better than “rain-cover.” Think about when the object appears, who uses it, or what it reminds you of.

Let the explanation be the punchline

If the word is “vacuum cleaner,” someone might say “Sunday.” That sounds suspicious until they explain their weekend cleaning routine. Allow a short explanation after clues to give odd answers a chance. Keep the tone playful; an accusation is part of the game.

If you are the imposter

Listen for the broad category before committing to a detail. When everyone mentions a time of day, a related routine can be a safer bluff than a very specific object. Repeating another player’s clue too closely is easy to notice, so take a small step sideways.

Avoid unfair clues

  • No rhymes, initials or translations that reveal the secret directly.
  • No inside jokes that exclude most of the room.
  • No teasing based on someone’s accent, background or reading speed.
  • No switching the agreed rules midway through the round.

Make the next round different

Change topics, switch to Undercover or turn hints off. Custom words about your group’s shared hobbies can create better moments than simply making every word obscure. Start with our word ideas and adapt them to your friends.
