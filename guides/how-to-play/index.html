THE ALIBI PLAYBOOK

A secret word. A suspiciously good time.

Published by Imposter Play · How this guide is made

Alibi is a social deduction word game for friends in the same room. Most players know the secret word. A few have to bluff. You need three to twenty people, one phone, and about five minutes.

The setup

Add everyone’s name, pick a few topics and choose your imposter count. Start with one imposter for your first game. In Classic mode, hints give the imposter a broad association rather than the actual word. Turn hints off when your group wants a tougher challenge.

1. Check your private card

Pass the phone to the named player. They tap to reveal their card, remember it and hide it before handing the phone over. Civilians see the same word. Classic imposters see their role and, if enabled, a hint. Never read the card aloud.

2. Give one-word clues

The game picks a starting player. Continue around the circle. If the word is “umbrella,” a clue like “forecast” suggests that you know it without spelling it out. Avoid the secret word itself, translations of it and obvious variations.

3. Discuss the suspicious answers

A vague clue does not automatically mean someone is an imposter. Ask why an answer fits. Imposters can use the discussion to build a convincing explanation. If the timer is running, pause it whenever the group needs a break.

4. Make a group accusation

When you are ready, choose exactly as many suspects as there are imposters. Discuss until the group agrees; this edition uses a shared group decision rather than individual secret ballots. The civilians win only if every chosen suspect is an imposter. Otherwise, the imposters win the round.

House rules worth agreeing on

  • Allow one sentence of explanation after each clue, or save explanations for discussion.
  • Skip clues that rely on private jokes only two people understand.
  • Keep the same language for a round unless everyone agrees otherwise.
  • Do not peek at the phone while someone else checks their role.

Alibi does not use an instant secret-word guessing rule. Win through the final group accusation. For a different challenge, try Undercover mode, where a player gets a related word without knowing their role.
